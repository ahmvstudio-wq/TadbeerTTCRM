# TTT Outreach Operating System
## How to Book Meetings and Close Deals in Oman

---

## Before Anything Else: Understand Where You Are

This is Oman. Not Silicon Valley. Not London. Not Dubai.

In Oman, business runs on relationships. Not LinkedIn automations, not cold email sequences, not "growth hacking." Relationships.

An Omani business owner — whether she runs an aesthetic clinic in Qurum or he runs a coffee roastery from his villa in Bausher — will not respond to a stranger pitching services. They get those messages constantly. From agencies in India, from freelancers in Pakistan, from local competitors. Every single day. They ignore all of them.

What they *will* respond to is a person who seems genuine, who noticed something specific about their business, who isn't trying to sell them anything in the first message, and who comes across as someone worth knowing.

That's not a sales technique. That's how Omani society works. Business is personal here. People work with people they like and trust. The concept of *Aman* (trust, safety, reliability) isn't a corporate buzzword — it's how decisions get made. The concept of *Wasta* (connections, social capital) isn't corruption — it's how doors open.

So the entire purpose of this operating system is to help you do one thing well:

**Start genuine conversations with business owners who have problems you can help with, and earn enough trust that they want to hear what you can do.**

That's it. Everything else — the meeting, the proposal, the deal — flows from that.

---

## What Omani Business Owners Actually Care About

Before we get into categories, let's be honest about what matters to the person on the other side of the WhatsApp message.

**They want more customers.** That's it. More patients, more orders, more bookings, more students. Revenue. Growth. Market visibility. They want their phone ringing with new business.

They do *not* want:
- "AI-powered automation solutions"
- "Digital transformation"  
- "Workflow optimization"
- "Intelligent booking ecosystems"

They want more customers. They want their Instagram to generate actual revenue, not just likes. They want to show up when someone Googles their service. They want the phone to stop going unanswered. They want their name to be the first one that comes up when someone in their neighbourhood needs what they offer.

**TTT's job is to deliver that.** The technology, the automation, the systems — those are *how* we deliver it. They're not the product. The product is growth.

Position everything around marketing, visibility, customer acquisition, and revenue. If a system or automation is involved, explain it as the mechanism that makes the marketing work — not as the thing you're selling.

---

## The Gate-Opener Philosophy

The first message you send to any prospect in Oman has one job: **get them to reply.**

Not to book a meeting. Not to agree to a demo. Not to understand your full service offering. Just to reply.

Because once they reply, you're in a conversation. And conversations in Oman naturally lead to coffee. And coffee leads to trust. And trust leads to business.

### What a good gate-opener looks like:
- It's short (3–5 sentences maximum)
- It references something *specific* about their business (not generic flattery)
- It sounds like a real person, not an agency
- It has zero sales language
- It asks a genuine question or makes a genuine observation
- It does not mention TTT, your services, or anything you're selling

### What a bad gate-opener looks like:
- "Hi, we are TTT and we specialise in digital solutions for businesses in Oman..."
- "I noticed your clinic could benefit from our AI-powered booking system..."
- "We help businesses like yours increase revenue by 300%..."
- Anything with the words "solutions", "leverage", "cutting-edge", or "state-of-the-art"

### The human test:
Before sending any message, read it out loud and ask: *"Would I send this to a friend's business? Does it sound like a human being wrote it?"*

If it sounds like it came from an agency, delete it and start over.

## The Conversation Arc

Every prospect relationship in Oman follows this arc. You cannot skip stages.

```
Stage 1: GATE-OPENER
   You notice something about their business. You reach out with a genuine,
   zero-agenda comment or question. You sound like a person, not a company.
        ↓
Stage 2: WARM-UP
   They reply. You continue the conversation naturally. You ask about their
   business. You share something useful. You establish yourself as someone
   who understands their world. You still don't pitch.
        ↓
Stage 3: THE OPENING
   At some point in the conversation — naturally, not forced — an opening
   appears. They mention a frustration. They ask what you do. They say
   something that reveals a gap. Now you can share a relevant insight.
        ↓
Stage 4: THE COFFEE
   You suggest meeting. Not a "sales meeting" or a "demo call." A coffee.
   A brief sit-down. "I'd love to hear more about how you're handling [thing].
   Can I buy you a coffee sometime this week?"
        ↓
Stage 5: THE MEETING
   In person. You listen more than you talk. You ask smart questions. You
   understand their actual situation. You share relevant examples of how
   you've helped (honestly). You leave them thinking about the conversation.
        ↓
Stage 6: THE FOLLOW-UP
   You send something useful. Not a proposal. Something they can use.
   A quick insight, a comparison, a recommendation. Something that proves
   you were listening and that you know your stuff.
        ↓
Stage 7: THE PROPOSAL
   Only after they've said or implied "this sounds interesting, what would
   it look like?" do you put together a proposal. Not before.
```

**How long does this take?** For a small business owner who's actively feeling pain — maybe 3 to 7 days from gate-opener to coffee. For a bigger operation with multiple stakeholders — 2 to 4 weeks. For a corporate or hospitality group — potentially 4 to 8 weeks.

Patience is not optional here. It's the strategy.

# 1. Aesthetic & Dermatology Clinics

### Who they are

These are private cosmetic and dermatology clinics scattered across Muscat's commercial zones — Qurum, Al Khuwair, Bausher, Mawaleh, Shatti Al Qurum — and increasingly in Sohar and Salalah. The owner is almost always a doctor. Usually a dermatologist or cosmetic physician. They invested serious money in equipment (lasers, IPL, injectables) and built a beautiful clinic space. Their Instagram looks premium.

Behind the scenes, the operation is messy. One receptionist manages the entire front desk — answering calls, greeting walk-ins, and responding to WhatsApp messages on a single phone. When a treatment post goes viral on Instagram, 30 people DM "price?" and half of them never get a reply.

### What they actually want

More patients. Specifically: more high-ticket patients booking cosmetic procedures like fillers, skin boosters, laser treatments, and veneers. They want a full appointment book. They want to stop losing potential patients to the new clinic that just opened down the road.

They don't know they have a "lead management problem." They know they're busy but revenue doesn't match the effort. They know patients cancel and don't show up. They know their Google presence is weak compared to competitors.

### Who to talk to

**The owner-doctor.** Always. The practice manager or receptionist cannot approve anything. The doctor is the decision-maker, the investor, and the person who feels the pain of empty treatment rooms.

Getting to the doctor: follow their *personal* Instagram (many doctors maintain a personal clinical account separate from the clinic account). Engage with their content genuinely over a few days before reaching out.

### Gate-openers that work

**Instagram DM to the doctor's personal clinical account:**
> "Dr [Name], your work on the skin rejuvenation cases you posted last week is really impressive — the results speak for themselves. Quick question: do most of your new patients find you through Instagram or through Google search? I'm curious because I've been looking at how aesthetic clinics in Muscat are growing their patient base and the patterns are really interesting."

**Why this works:** It's a genuine compliment on specific clinical work (not generic "your page looks great"). The question is interesting and non-threatening. It positions you as someone studying the industry, not selling something.

**WhatsApp to the clinic's public number (lower-touch, higher volume):**
> "Ahlan, I came across [Clinic Name] on Instagram and noticed you guys are really active with your content. I was curious — are you seeing most new patient inquiries come through Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, or Google? I work in the healthcare marketing space in Muscat and I'm always trying to understand what's actually working for clinics here."

**Why this works:** It sounds like genuine market curiosity. It flatters them by acknowledging their content effort. The question is easy to answer and non-invasive.

### What you're NOT saying

You're not mentioning TTT. You're not mentioning AI. You're not mentioning booking systems. You're not mentioning SEO. You're having a conversation.

### The warm-up

When they reply (and if your message was genuine, many will), continue naturally:

> "That's interesting — Instagram seems to be the biggest driver for most aesthetic clinics here. The challenge I keep hearing from clinic owners is that the inquiries come in, but converting DMs into actual booked appointments is where things get tricky. Is that something you've noticed too, or have you guys figured out a good system for it?"

This is where *they* bring up the pain. You didn't tell them they have a problem. They told you.

### The opening

When they say something like "Yeah, we lose a lot of people in the DM stage" or "Our receptionist can't keep up with messages" — *that's* your opening. Not before.

> "I hear that a lot actually. A few clinics I've been talking to in Muscat are testing some interesting approaches to handle that volume without adding more reception staff. Would be happy to share what I've seen over a quick coffee if you're ever free."

### What to observe before reaching out

Before you message any aesthetic clinic, spend 5 minutes checking these things. You don't need to "mystery shop" them — just look at what's publicly visible:

- **Instagram:** Are people commenting "price?" or "how much?" on posts? Are those comments answered? How quickly?
- **Google Maps:** Search "aesthetic clinic [their area]". Do they show up? How many reviews? What rating? Are negative reviews answered?
- **Their bio link:** Does it go to a website with booking? Or just an Instagram page? Or a WhatsApp number?
- **Meta Ad Library:** Are they running paid Instagram/Facebook ads? (Search ads.facebook.com for their clinic name)

These observations become your personalisation. Never mention that you "audited" them. Just naturally reference what you noticed.

### The problems that matter (in order of what they care about)

1. **"I spend money on Instagram content but I don't know how many patients it actually brings in."** This is the #1 thing. Marketing accountability. They're spending on content, maybe influencers, maybe ads — but they can't connect it to appointments. *TTT can solve this with proper marketing attribution, landing pages, and conversion tracking.*

2. **"New clinics keep opening and competing on price."** Market competition anxiety. They want to stand out. *TTT can solve this with Google local SEO dominance, reputation management, and content strategy that positions the doctor as an authority.*

3. **"Patients book but don't show up, and we can't fill the slot."** No-shows. They feel this every day. *TTT can solve this with automated WhatsApp appointment reminders and confirmation workflows.*

4. **"We get tons of DMs and WhatsApp messages but our receptionist can't keep up."** Inquiry overload. *TTT can solve this with WhatsApp triage systems and structured inquiry flows.*

5. **"I know we should be on Google but I don't know where to start."** SEO awareness without action. *TTT can solve this with Google Business Profile optimization and local SEO.*

### What to offer for free

**The most valuable free offer for an aesthetic clinic is showing them what happens when someone in their area searches Google for their service.**

This costs you 5 minutes. Open Google Maps, search "skin clinic [their area]" or "botox [their city]", screenshot the results. Show them where they rank (or don't rank). Show them who ranks above them. Show them their competitor's review count vs. theirs.

This is not a "formal audit." It's a conversation tool. "Hey, I was looking at this earlier and thought you'd find it interesting..."

### When to stop pursuing

- The doctor is not commercially minded (some are purely clinical and have no interest in business growth)
- The clinic is part of a hospital group with centralised procurement
- They already have a sophisticated setup with online booking, active SEO, and structured marketing
- After 3 genuine touchpoints with no response, move on. You can come back in 60 days with something new.

# 2. Dental Clinics

### Who they are

Private dental practices in Muscat, Sohar, Salalah, and Nizwa. Range from 2-chair family practices to 6-chair polyclinics offering everything from routine cleanings to high-ticket cosmetic work (veneers, implants, clear aligners).

The owner is a dentist. They care deeply about clinical quality. They're less Instagram-savvy than aesthetic clinic owners. Their patient acquisition historically relies on word-of-mouth, insurance directories, and local reputation.

Their biggest operational frustration: **phones.** The front desk phone never stops ringing, and when it does, it goes unanswered because the receptionist is checking in a patient. Meanwhile, prospective patients are calling 3 clinics and booking with whichever one picks up.

### What they actually want

A full appointment book. Fewer cancelled appointments. More high-ticket cosmetic cases (veneers, implants, aligners — these are the profitable ones). Better Google visibility so that when someone in their neighbourhood searches for a dentist, they show up first.

### Who to talk to

The owner-dentist. In larger practices, the clinic manager might champion internally, but the dentist signs off.

Dental clinic owners are harder to reach via Instagram DM than aesthetic doctors. They're more responsive to **phone calls** (ironically, since their patients can't reach them by phone either) and **WhatsApp messages** that reference something concrete about their practice.

### Gate-openers that work

**WhatsApp to the clinic's business number:**
> "Ahlan, quick question — I was looking for a dental clinic in [their area] on Google and noticed [Clinic Name] comes up, but [Competitor Name] seems to be ranking higher and has about 3 times the reviews. I work with local healthcare businesses on their Google visibility and was curious whether that's something on your radar or if most of your patients still come through referrals?"

**Why this works:** It's factual and verifiable. You're not insulting them — you're pointing out a competitive dynamic they can check themselves. The question is genuine.

**Phone call (for dental, this channel works):**
> "Ahlan, my name is [Name]. I was looking at dental practices in [area] and noticed [Clinic Name] has excellent patient feedback but seems to be missing from some of the top Google search results compared to a few competitors nearby. I work with dental practices on improving their local search visibility. Is the doctor available for a brief chat, or would you prefer I send a WhatsApp message with more details?"

**Why this works:** You've spoken to the gatekeeper (receptionist) respectfully. You've given a concrete, non-threatening reason. You've offered their preferred follow-up channel.

### The problems that matter

1. **"When someone searches for a dentist near us on Google, our competitors show up first."** This is the killer. They know Google matters but haven't done anything about it. *TTT can solve this with Google Business Profile optimisation, local SEO, and review generation workflows.*

2. **"Patients cancel last minute or just don't show up."** Empty chair hours. The dentist is paying for the chair, the assistant, the room — whether a patient is in it or not. *TTT can solve this with automated WhatsApp confirmation and reminder sequences.*

3. **"We do great implant and veneer work but nobody knows about it."** They have high-ticket cosmetic capabilities that aren't marketed. *TTT can solve this with targeted digital marketing campaigns and cosmetic-specific landing pages.*

4. **"Our phone is always busy and patients complain they can't get through."** Phone congestion during peak hours. *TTT can solve this with WhatsApp-based appointment scheduling and inquiry handling.*

### What to offer for free

Google Maps comparison. Search their primary keywords, screenshot where they appear vs. competitors, show review counts side by side. Takes 5 minutes. Creates an immediate "I need to fix this" reaction.

### When to stop pursuing

- Government-run dental departments
- Owner-dentist is within 2–3 years of retirement with no succession plan
- They're already working with a competent marketing team and have strong Google presence
- After 3 genuine touches with no response

# 3. Social-Commerce & DTC Brands

This is a broad category, so let's be honest about it. An abaya designer in Bausher and a specialty coffee roaster in Al Khuwair have very different businesses. What they share is this: **they sell physical products, they acquire customers through social media, and their order process runs through WhatsApp.**

### Subsegment A: Fashion & Abayas

**Who they are:** Omani women (usually) designing and selling abayas, modest fashion, or contemporary women's wear. Many operate from home. Followers range from 2,000 to 100,000+. Their entire business runs through Instagram posts and WhatsApp conversations.

**What they want:** More orders. Smoother ordering during collection drops. The ability to sell beyond Muscat. A professional online store that matches their brand quality.

**Gate-opener:**
> "[Founder Name], your new collection is stunning — especially the embroidered cuff detailing. I'm curious, when you launch a new collection and get 50+ DMs in an evening, how do you manage all the sizing and ordering conversations? I imagine it gets intense."

### Subsegment B: Specialty Coffee & Gourmet Food

**Who they are:** Oman's growing artisan food and drink scene. Coffee roasters, specialty date and halwa packagers, honey producers, artisanal chocolate makers. Usually a passionate founder, small team, Instagram-forward.

**What they want:** More online orders. Corporate gifting clients. The ability to handle seasonal spikes (Ramadan, Eid, National Day) without chaos.

**Gate-opener:**
> "[Brand Name], I just tried your [specific product] — genuinely excellent. Quick question: do you find that most of your repeat orders come from customers reaching out again on WhatsApp, or do you have a system that reminds them when they might be running low?"

### Subsegment C: Beauty, Skincare & Fragrance

**Who they are:** Homegrown Omani brands creating natural skincare, perfumes, or oudh products. Often a single founder with a compelling brand story.

**What they want:** Growth beyond Instagram followers. Proper e-commerce so customers outside Muscat can buy easily. Brand legitimacy and trust badges.

**Gate-opener:**
> "[Founder Name], your brand story is really compelling — the sourcing of [specific ingredient] from [specific Omani region] is a real differentiator. Are most of your orders coming from within Muscat, or are you getting demand from other parts of Oman and the GCC too?"

### Common problems across all DTC subsegments

1. **"I'm spending my entire evening answering the same questions on WhatsApp."** The founder is the salesperson, the customer service, and the fulfilment team. This is the #1 bottleneck to growth. *TTT can solve this with structured WhatsApp ordering flows and marketing automation.*

2. **"I have no idea which influencer campaign actually drove sales."** They spend on influencers but can't attribute revenue. *TTT can solve this with tracked promotional links and conversion analytics.*

3. **"I'm still asking customers to send bank transfer screenshots."** Manual payment verification is crushing their ability to scale. *TTT can solve this with Thawani Pay integration and proper e-commerce checkout.*

4. **"I get tonnes of orders during Ramadan and things fall apart."** Seasonal chaos. *TTT can solve this with pre-order systems, automated inventory management, and scalable checkout flows.*

### What to offer for free

The most powerful thing you can do for a DTC founder is place a test order and document the experience. Not as a "mystery audit" — just as a genuine customer. Then share what you noticed conversationally: "Hey, I ordered from you last week — great product by the way. I noticed the ordering process took about 7 back-and-forth messages. Have you thought about setting up a direct checkout link?"

### When to stop pursuing

- Hobby sellers doing fewer than 10 orders per month
- Founders who are resistant to any digital payment adoption
- Brands with no active social content (dormant accounts)
- After 3 genuine touches with no response

# 4. Professional Training & Education

This category splits into two genuinely different businesses that happen to share a sector label.

### Subsegment A: Vocational & Professional Certification Institutes

**Who they are:** Private training institutes licensed by the Ministry of Labour. They deliver certifications in project management (PMP), health and safety (NEBOSH), accounting (ACCA), IT certifications, and technical trades. Their students are working professionals seeking career advancement and companies sending staff for mandatory upskilling.

The government's 1.2% national training levy (Circular No. 6/2025) has created a massive funding pool for corporate training, meaning there's real money on the table.

**What they want:** Full batches. They schedule a 15-person NEBOSH class and 8 people sign up. They either run the batch at a loss or postpone it and frustrate the 8 who already paid. They want consistent enrolment, and they want corporate clients sending them 20-person bulk bookings.

**Who to talk to:** The institute director or the head of business development.

**Gate-opener:**
> "Ahlan [Director Name], I saw your announcement for the upcoming [PMP / NEBOSH / specific course] batch in [month]. How's enrolment looking so far? I work with training providers in Oman on their student acquisition and I'm always curious whether the pipeline is looking healthy or whether it's the usual last-week scramble to fill seats."

**Why this works:** It acknowledges a reality they experience every batch cycle. The "last-week scramble" is universally relatable for training institute directors. It positions you as an insider who understands their world.

### Subsegment B: Study-Abroad Consultancies

**Who they are:** Education agencies that place Omani students in universities in the UK, US, Malaysia, Australia, and Europe. They earn commission from the university for each enrolled student.

**What they want:** More qualified student inquiries, better application tracking so nothing falls through the cracks, and improved parent communication (parents are heavily involved in the decision in Oman).

**Gate-opener:**
> "Ahlan [Agency Name], I noticed you place students in [specific university/country]. With the September intake coming up, how are you finding the student inquiry volume this year compared to last? I'm exploring how education consultancies in Oman are managing their student pipelines and would love to hear your perspective."

### Common problems across training/education

1. **"We spend on ads but the leads don't convert."** They're running Instagram or Google ads. Leads come in. But the admissions team takes too long to respond, and the prospective student has already contacted 3 other institutes. *TTT can solve this with rapid-response WhatsApp admissions flows and landing page optimisation.*

2. **"We host free workshops to attract students but most people who register don't show up."** Demo class ghosting. *TTT can solve this with automated WhatsApp event reminders.*

3. **"Corporate clients want proposals and we take a week to put one together."** Slow B2B proposal turnaround losing enterprise contracts. *TTT can help streamline proposal creation and B2B sales pipeline management.*

### What to offer for free

Submit an inquiry as a prospective student through their ad or website. Document how long they take to respond and what the experience is like. Share this conversationally: "I wanted to understand your enrolment journey, so I submitted an inquiry as a test. Here's what I experienced..."

### When to stop pursuing

- Unaccredited tutoring centres
- Institutes with no active student acquisition effort (no ads, no social media)
- Single-instructor freelance trainers
- After 3 genuine touches with no response

# 5. Hospitality & Premium F&B

This also splits into two related but distinct businesses.

### Subsegment A: Independent Boutique Hotels & Resorts

**Who they are:** Luxury independent properties in Jebel Akhdar, Jebel Shams, Wahiba Sands, Salalah, and boutique hotels in Muscat. Not the Sheratons and Hiltons — those have corporate procurement. The independents. Often owned by Omani families or investors.

**What they want:** More direct bookings. Every booking that comes through Booking.com costs them 15–25% in commission. They know this. They hate it. But their own website doesn't have a proper booking engine, and guests default to the OTA because it's easier.

**Who to talk to:** The General Manager. In family-owned properties, the owner.

**Gate-opener:**
> "Ahlan [GM Name], I stayed at / dined at / visited [Property Name] recently and the experience was genuinely excellent — [mention something specific: the view, the service, a particular dish]. I noticed when I was looking to book, the easiest option was through Booking.com rather than your direct website. I'm curious — is direct booking something you're actively working on, or is the OTA channel working well enough for now?"

**Why this works:** It's an honest observation from a genuine guest perspective. The OTA commission question is one that every independent hotelier has strong feelings about.

### Subsegment B: Fine Dining & Upscale F&B

**Who they are:** High-end restaurants, chef-driven concepts, upscale lounges, and experiential dining venues in Muscat and Salalah. Average cover spend OMR 15–60+.

**What they want:** Full tables on weekends (they often are). Full tables on weekdays (they usually aren't). Fewer no-shows. Better handling of private event and corporate dining inquiries.

**Gate-opener:**
> "Ahlan [Manager/Chef Name], I had an incredible meal at [Restaurant Name] last [day] — the [specific dish] was outstanding. I'm curious, do you find that your weekday traffic is significantly different from the weekend crowd? I've been looking at how premium restaurants in Muscat manage that midweek gap and it's a fascinating challenge."

### Common problems across hospitality/F&B

1. **"We're paying Booking.com/Talabat a fortune in commissions."** OTA dependency. *TTT can solve this with direct booking engine development and marketing to drive direct guest acquisition.*

2. **"Tables are booked for Friday night and then people don't show up."** Fine dining no-shows. *TTT can solve this with WhatsApp confirmation workflows.*

3. **"Sunday to Wednesday the restaurant is half empty."** Midweek slump. *TTT can solve this with targeted marketing campaigns to past guests and local corporate outreach.*

4. **"We get event inquiries but we're slow to respond with proposals."** Lost private dining and corporate events. *TTT can help streamline event inquiry handling and proposal turnaround.*

### What to offer for free

Dine there. Experience the reservation process. Note what works and what doesn't. Have a genuine conversation about what you observed — not as a formal "audit" but as a guest sharing feedback that happens to have commercial implications.

### When to stop pursuing

- International chain hotels with corporate IT mandates
- Budget fast-food restaurants
- Properties with existing sophisticated direct booking infrastructure
- After 3 genuine touches with no response

# THE DAILY RHYTHM

Here's how a productive day of TTT outreach actually looks in Oman:

### Morning (9:00 – 11:00): Research & Prepare
- Identify 5–8 new prospects across your active categories
- For each one, spend 5 minutes checking their Instagram, Google Maps presence, and website
- Note one specific, genuine observation about each business
- Draft personalised gate-openers (not templates — actual personalised messages)
- Queue them for sending at optimal times

### Mid-Morning (11:00 – 13:00): Outreach & Conversations
- Send gate-openers via the appropriate channel (Instagram DM, WhatsApp, phone)
- Respond to any overnight replies with genuine, conversation-continuing messages
- Follow up on warm conversations that are 2–3 days old (add value, don't just "check in")
- **Optimal WhatsApp/DM timing:** 10:00 – 12:00 and 15:00 – 17:00 and 19:00 – 21:00

### Afternoon (15:00 – 17:00): Meetings & Follow-ups
- This is when in-person coffees happen
- Follow up on coffees within 24 hours with a useful insight or resource
- Prepare any free value assets (Google visibility screenshots, order experience notes)
- Update your pipeline notes

### Evening (19:00 – 21:00): Social Engagement
- This is when DTC founders and clinic owners are active on Instagram
- Engage genuinely with their content (thoughtful comments, not emoji reactions)
- Send evening-appropriate gate-openers to fashion/DTC founders (they're often online after their kids are asleep, processing orders)
- Respond to any late-afternoon replies

### What you're tracking (honestly, not obsessively)

| What to Measure | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gate-openers sent this week | Are you doing enough outreach? |
| Replies received | Are your messages resonating? |
| Conversations past 3 exchanges | Are you building relationships? |
| Coffees/meetings booked | Are conversations converting to face time? |
| Proposals requested | Are meetings leading to commercial interest? |
| Deals closed | Are proposals converting? |

If your reply rate is below 15%, your messages sound too salesy. Rewrite them.

If you're getting replies but not booking meetings, you're staying in chat too long. Move the conversation to coffee sooner.

If you're booking meetings but not getting proposals requested, you're not listening enough in meetings. Ask more questions about their business before sharing what you do.

# HOW TO POSITION TTT IN CONVERSATION

When the moment comes — and it *will* come naturally if you've done the earlier stages right — here's how to talk about what TTT does.

### Don't say:
"We offer AI-powered digital solutions for businesses..."

### Do say:
"We help businesses in Oman get more customers through better marketing and smarter systems. For clinics, that usually means showing up on Google when patients search, and making sure every inquiry actually turns into an appointment. For brands, it's about turning Instagram followers into paying customers without the WhatsApp chaos."

### The positioning framework:
1. **Start with their world:** "Most [aesthetic clinics / dental practices / coffee brands / hotels] in Oman face [specific challenge they mentioned]..."
2. **Name the gap:** "The gap is usually between the marketing that brings people in and the system that turns them into [patients / orders / bookings]..."
3. **Describe the outcome:** "What we do is close that gap — more [patients / orders / bookings] from the same effort..."
4. **Only then describe the mechanism:** "We do that through a combination of [marketing campaigns / Google visibility / WhatsApp handling / online ordering / direct booking] depending on what makes sense for the business."

### What TTT can honestly offer right now:
- **Digital marketing management** (Meta ads, Google ads, LinkedIn campaigns)
- **Google Business Profile optimisation and local SEO**
- **Website development and landing pages** that actually convert
- **CRM setup and customer database structuring**
- **WhatsApp Business configuration** with structured inquiry flows
- **Online booking and scheduling system setup**
- **Social media marketing strategy and execution**
- **Human capital and SOP development**

### What TTT can build with project-specific scoping:
- Custom WhatsApp conversational assistants
- Direct booking engines for hospitality
- E-commerce storefronts with local payment integration (Thawani Pay)
- Internal knowledge bases and staff training tools
- Automated appointment reminders and customer follow-up sequences

### What requires external partners:
- Direct courier logistics integrations (Asyad Express)
- Fiscal e-invoicing (Fawtara compliance)
- Deep EMR/PMS database synchronisation

Be honest about these boundaries. An Omani business owner will trust you *more* for being straightforward about what you can and can't do than for pretending you do everything.

# WHEN THEY SAY "HOW MUCH?"

This will come up. Handle it with honesty, not evasion.

> "It depends on what makes sense for your business. Some of our clients work with us on a monthly marketing and systems retainer. Others prefer a project-based approach where we build something specific and hand it over. I'd rather understand your situation properly before throwing out a number — can we sit down and figure out what would actually move the needle for you?"

Do not quote prices in chat. Do not quote prices before a proper conversation. Do not fabricate package prices. The pricing conversation happens face-to-face, after discovery, based on the actual scope.

# WHEN THEY SAY "NOT INTERESTED"

Respect it. Immediately.

> "Completely understood. If anything changes or if you'd like a second opinion on anything in the future, I'm always around. Wishing you and the team continued success."

Then mark them as "Not Now" in your pipeline and revisit in 60–90 days with something genuinely new to share (not a re-pitch).

In Oman, a "no" today is not a "no" forever. But a pushy follow-up after a "no" is a permanent door closure.

---

# WHEN THEY SAY "WE ALREADY WORK WITH AN AGENCY"

This is common and usually true.

> "That's great — having someone handling [their marketing / their social media] is important. I'm not suggesting replacing anyone. I'm more curious about whether you're seeing the results you're hoping for in terms of actual [patients / orders / bookings] coming through. If things are working well, that's brilliant and I'd just like to understand what's working for you. If there are gaps, happy to share some thoughts."

The goal is not to badmouth their existing agency. The goal is to understand whether the existing agency is delivering *commercial results* (more customers) or just *activity* (more posts). If it's the latter, the conversation will naturally reveal the opportunity.

---

# FINAL PRINCIPLES

1. **Every message must sound like it was written by a human being who actually looked at this specific business.** No templates. No copy-paste. Every message references something concrete.

2. **The first message has zero agenda.** It's a genuine conversation starter. Not a pitch. Not an intro to your services. A conversation.

3. **Marketing and customer acquisition come first in every positioning.** Systems and automation are the engine. Growth is the car. Sell the car, not the engine.

4. **Never claim results you haven't delivered.** No fake audits, no invented benchmarks, no promised percentages. "Here's what we've seen work" is fine. "We guarantee a 40% increase" is not.

5. **Respect the pace.** Omani business owners will make decisions when they're ready. Pushing faster doesn't work — it closes doors.

6. **Be present in the market.** Attend events, join business groups, have coffee in the places where your prospects spend time. Oman is small enough that being visibly present and genuinely helpful builds reputation faster than any digital outreach campaign.

7. **Follow up with value, not pressure.** Every follow-up message should give them something useful — a relevant observation, a market insight, a genuine compliment on something new they've done. Never send "just checking in" or "following up on my previous message."

8. **Know when to stop.** Three genuine, personalised, value-adding touches with no response = move on. You can revisit in 60 days. Pushing past three creates negative associations with TTT's name.
